No configuration shows an excess: the largest $|z|$ is \zAbsMaxNone{} without extra cuts and \zAbsMaxBoth{} with both
pair requirements; no value has $|z|>2$. Per configuration the r.m.s.\ of the 12 $z$ values is \zRmsNone{} (no extra
cut), \zRmsIntt{} (INTT), \zRmsColl{} (same collision) and \zRmsBoth{} (both), and \zPosNone{} of 12 excesses are
positive without extra cuts. The narrow-peak fit gives $|z_{\rm narrow}|\leq\nzAbsMaxNone$. An injected narrow peak is
recovered at \recMinNone--\recMaxNone\% in every selection (\chkInjNone, \chkInjBoth).

\paragraph{Effect of the PV versions.} The refits change which candidates are selected (Table~\ref{tab:counts}); for all \pt,
tight, the original PV selects \nsOrigAllTNone, the pair refit \nsPairAllTNone{} and the KF fit \nsKfAllTNone{}
candidates (the KF numbers are low because of its larger $\sigma_L$, Sec.~\ref{sec:splithalf}). In none of the versions
does an excess appear at the \Dzero{} mass.

\paragraph{Background model.} Table~\ref{tab:model} gives the polynomial $\chi^2$/ndf of $S/P$ and the broad-component
$\Delta\chi^2$ for the raw ratio $S/C$ and for $S/P$. In the pointing selections, where the raw ratio has a broad structure, the weighting
reduces its $\Delta\chi^2$ substantially (uncorrected rows of Table~\ref{tab:model}); in the tight selections the broad
structure is small with and without weighting. The remaining $\chi^2$/ndf of up to \chiMaxNone{} shows that the prediction does not describe the data to the
statistical precision everywhere. In the tight selections $S/P$ still falls by about 20\% from 1.6 to 2.0\gevcc{}
(read by eye from Fig.~\ref{fig:uncorrT}), which the polynomial absorbs.
