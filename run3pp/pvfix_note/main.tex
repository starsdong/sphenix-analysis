\title{Primary-vertex bias in the KFParticle \Dzero{} reconstruction:\\
fixed production, momentum-scale correction and charm searches\\
in the sPHENIX Run-3 p+p data (ana581\_2026p005\_v001)\\[4pt]
\large Analysis note \texttt{d0-pvfix}: full \Dzero{} sample and 3-prong charm (draft)}
\author{X.~Dong (dongxs) \quad -- \quad draft prepared with Hermes}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The stock KFParticle reconstruction measures pointing and decay length of charm candidates with respect to the stored
primary vertex (PV), which was found with the candidate's daughters among its tracks; the PV is pulled towards the decay
and displaced candidates are lost in the job. A modified KFParticle (branch \texttt{d0-pvfix}, \nCommits{} commits)
refits the PV without the daughters with two methods (a re-implementation of the production pair-average algorithm and a
KFParticle vertex fit with calibrated track errors), applies the production cuts with any of the three PVs, and
reproduces the stock candidate set exactly. It was run on the \Dzero$\to K^-\pi^+$ channel of the sPHENIX Run-3 p+p
track DSTs: \jobsOkTwo{} + \jobsOkNin{} jobs, all OK, \eventsAll\,M events, \candAllStock\,M stock candidates
reproduced with no mismatching file. Removing the daughters moves the PV by a median of \shPairTwoMed\um{} in the
transverse plane and adds 35\% more candidates. A momentum-scale correction from a separate calibration narrows the
\Ks{} peak from \vKsAllSigRc{} to \vKsAllSigKc\mev; it is applied as a separate calculation.

The \Dzero{} search compares pointing with non-pointing candidates weighted with the measured pointing probability, in
12 selections (2 \pt{} ranges $\times$ 2 pointing selections $\times$ 3 PV versions), with and without the correction and
two extra pair requirements. Moving the search window over the mass range shows that the background model fluctuates
more than the statistical errors (r.m.s.\ of the local $z$ 0.7--4.3); significances are also given divided by that
r.m.s.\ ($z_{\rm c}$). In the full sample the 10\% excesses of the loose selections disappear. The original-PV,
$\pt>1.5\gevc$, tight selection keeps an excess of \exOrigHiTFuKc{} $\pm$ \eexOrigHiTFuKc{} candidates ($z_{\rm c}\approx
\zcOrigHiTFuKc$, local), stable over three run groups but not confirmed by the refitted PVs; the 5$\sigma$ KF-fit tight
excesses are broader than a \Dzero{} peak and absent in one run group. A search in a 10\% stock-PV production of
D$^+$, D$_s^+$ and $\Lambda_c^+$ finds positive D$^+$ excesses up to $z_{\rm c}\approx\chZcDpNoneKcAllP$ and nothing robust
in D$_s^+$ and $\Lambda_c^+$. \textbf{No charm signal is claimed.}

\textbf{Limitations:} statistical uncertainties only, with a rough model calibration; no simulation (expected yields,
widths, efficiencies unknown); fixed 15\mev{} narrow-peak width; no look-elsewhere correction; the momentum-scale map was
derived on runs included here, from vertex-constrained daughters, and is applied to unconstrained tracks; the 3-prong
production has the stock PV bias. Sections~\ref{sec:fixes}--\ref{sec:resultskc} document the method and the 10\% sample
on which it was developed; Sec.~\ref{sec:full} gives the full-sample \Dzero{} results, Sec.~\ref{sec:charm} the 3-prong
channels.
Boxes: \textcolor{blue!60!black}{blue} = choice made and why,
\textcolor{red!60!black}{red} = caveat / open point,
\textcolor{orange!80!black}{orange} = needs a decision by the analyser.
\end{abstract}
