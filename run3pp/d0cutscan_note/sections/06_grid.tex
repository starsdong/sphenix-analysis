\section{Grid scan on the 10\% skim}
\label{sec:grid}

The grid crosses DIRA $>0.99, 0.995, 0.998, 0.999$; \Ls{} $>2,3,4,5,7$; \Dzero{} $\pt>0,1,2,3\gevc$; PID on/off; cts on/off:
\gridN{} selections. Each is fitted with the fixed Gaussian at $m_{\mathrm{D}^0}$ for three assumed widths (10, 15,
22\,MeV); all \gridFitFifteen{} selections have enough entries to be fitted.

\paragraph{Result.} For 10 / 15 / 22\,MeV the mean $z$ is \gridMeanTen{} / \gridMeanFifteen{} / \gridMeanTwentyTwo{} and
the r.m.s.\ \gridRmsTen{} / \gridRmsFifteen{} / \gridRmsTwentyTwo; above $+2$: \gridPosTen{} / \gridPosFifteen{} /
\gridPosTwentyTwo, below $-2$: \gridNegTen{} / \gridNegFifteen{} / \gridNegTwentyTwo{} (\gridExpFifteen{} expected per
tail). Both tails are populated at similar rates and the width grows with the assumed peak width, which is the
signature of background mismodelling over the wider window rather than of a signal; a signal would shift the distribution
to positive $z$ for the selections that enrich it. The largest value, $z=\gridTopZ$ ($S=\gridTopS\pm\gridTopES$), is for
the loose selection DIRA$>0.995$, \Ls$>2$ without further cuts, where Sec.~\ref{sec:trial} shows that the background is not
described.

\caveat{The \gridN{} selections overlap strongly, so their $z$ values are correlated; the tail counts cannot be compared
with the Gaussian expectation as independent trials. The comparison is used only to show that positive and negative
excursions are equally frequent.}
